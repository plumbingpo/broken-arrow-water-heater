Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

Swapping a water heater looks simple until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. Electric units need a breaker and circuit that match the element wattage. Each tank needs a properly rated relief valve whose discharge pipe ends somewhere safe, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps to meet local code. Moving to tankless or a heat pump raises the stakes further, with larger gas lines, new vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. We size the heater by first-hour rating or flow rate, verify the fuel, venting, and electrical capacity, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
