How Bixby Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Bixby

Whatever type of water heater your Bixby home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units show error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets. We service all four types regularly and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
