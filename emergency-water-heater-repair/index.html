Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • Hot water far hotter than normal, a sign of a runaway thermostat or gas control
  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Emergency calls combine electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can leak exhaust into the room if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be fixed at the source, not muted. Blocking the discharge pipe, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are all common and dangerous mistakes.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and a truck full of the likely parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution instead of relying on a sniff test, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Just as important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. You get that answer before any work begins.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here's where to begin:

  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
  • Lift the relief valve lever once a year to confirm it opens and reseats, and call if it keeps dripping
  • Annual flushing keeps sediment from causing the rumbling and hot spots that lead to leaks
  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
