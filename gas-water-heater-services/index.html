Most gas water heaters in Broken Arrow, OK work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve shuts off the gas for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across Broken Arrow, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Safety always comes first: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and Broken Arrow Water Heater can take over from there.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • Hot water that runs out faster than it used to

Why Hire a Professional for Gas Water Heater Services?

Start with the hazards. Every gas water heater involves fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust that contains carbon monoxide. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and replacing them one by one is slow and costly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Drain sediment from the bottom of the tank once or twice a year
