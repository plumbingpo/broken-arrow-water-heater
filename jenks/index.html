A little caution before we arrive can head off a lot of water damage. For a leak, turn off the cold supply valve at the top of the tank, then cut the power at the breaker on an electric model or set the gas control to "off" on a gas model. Move boxes and anything valuable away from the tank and put down towels to slow the spread. Gas smells are never a wait-and-see situation: leave and call the gas utility from outside.

Keeping Your Jenks Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.
