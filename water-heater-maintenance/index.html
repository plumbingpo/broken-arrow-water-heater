The inside of a neglected water heater tells the whole story. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in Broken Arrow, OK, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

A Broken Arrow Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we test the elements and thermostats with a meter and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, verify the temperature setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

Tankless heaters skip the sediment bed but have a scale problem of their own. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually causing shutdowns. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the inlet screen, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures

Why Professional Water Heater Maintenance Matters

A few maintenance tasks suit homeowners, and many do not. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are another story. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Look around the base of the heater periodically for moisture
